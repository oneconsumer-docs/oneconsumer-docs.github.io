## What OneStep learns about you

### 💰 Your income reality

**OneStep asks:**
- What's your monthly in-hand salary? (not CTC)
- Do you have any other income?
- How stable is your income?

**Why it matters:**
Advice for someone earning ₹30,000 is different from someone earning ₹80,000. OneStep tailors recommendations to what you can actually afford.

**Example:**
₹30k salary → "Focus on zero-fee credit card, low limit"
₹80k salary → "You can consider cards with annual fee if benefits outweigh cost"

---

### 🏠 Your expense reality

**OneStep asks:**
- What's your rent? (if you're paying)
- What are your other main expenses? (food, transport, bills)
- Do you have existing EMIs or loans?

**Why it matters:**
Your expenses determine how much you have left for savings, investments, or new financial commitments.

**Example:**
₹40k salary, ₹20k rent → "Keep credit limit low. Focus on building emergency fund first."
₹40k salary, ₹8k rent → "You have more buffer. Can save more aggressively."

---

### 🌆 Your location reality

**OneStep asks:**
- Which city are you in?
- Are you planning to move?
- Do you travel regularly?

**Why it matters:**
Cost of living varies wildly. ₹40,000 goes very differently in Tier-1 vs Tier-2 cities.

**Example:**
₹40k in Mumbai → "Budget will be tight. Focus on essentials first."
₹40k in Indore → "More breathing room. Can save 20-25% comfortably."

---

### 🎯 Your goals reality

**OneStep asks:**
- What are you trying to achieve?
- Are you saving for something specific?
- What's your timeline?

**Why it matters:**
Advice changes based on whether you're saving for emergencies, a laptop in 6 months, or long-term wealth.

**Example:**
Goal: Emergency fund → "High-liquidity savings. Skip investing for now."
Goal: Laptop in 3 months → "Save ₹X monthly in savings account. Don't lock in FD."

---

### 📊 Your current financial situation

**OneStep asks:**
- Do you have emergency savings?
- Any existing credit cards or loans?
- Do you have a credit score?

**Why it matters:**
Where you are determines what you should do next.

**Example:**
No emergency fund → "Build that first before investing or big purchases."
Has emergency fund → "Now we can talk about investing or planned expenses."

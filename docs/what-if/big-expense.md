**Laptop. Phone. Bike. Furniture. Trip home.**

Big purchases are part of life. The question is: **how do you pay for them without wrecking your finances?**

Let's figure it out.

---

## Before you buy: The real questions

### Question 1: Do I actually need this now?

**Be honest:**
- **Need:** My laptop died. I work on it daily.
- **Want:** I want the newest phone even though mine works fine.

**Nothing wrong with wants.** Just be clear about what it is.

**Needs:** Plan and buy smartly.
**Wants:** Make sure you can afford it without hurting your finances.

---

### Question 2: Can I afford this?

**"Afford" doesn't mean "can I get a loan for this."**

**Afford means:**
- Can I pay for this without breaking my budget?
- Will this hurt my savings or emergency fund?
- Can I handle the EMI without stress?

**Ask OneStep:** "I earn ₹40k, rent is ₹15k. Can I afford a ₹60,000 laptop?"

---

### Question 3: How urgent is this purchase?

**Need it today:** Different strategy.
**Can wait 2-3 months:** Better options available.

**Urgency determines your approach.**

## Your options for big purchases

### Option 1: Pay cash (save and buy)

**How it works:**
Save ₹X per month for Y months. Then buy.

**Pros:**
✅ Zero interest
✅ Zero debt
✅ You own it outright
✅ No monthly EMI stress

**Cons:**
❌ You wait
❌ Need discipline to save
❌ Opportunity cost (if you needed it earlier)

**Best for:** Purchases you can wait 2-4 months for.

---

### Option 2: EMI (installment plan)

**How it works:**
Split ₹X purchase into monthly payments over 6-12 months. Pay interest.

**Pros:**
✅ Get it now
✅ Spread cost over time
✅ Easier on monthly budget

**Cons:**
❌ Pay interest (10-18% typically)
❌ Monthly commitment
❌ Total cost is higher

**Best for:** Urgent needs when you can afford monthly EMI comfortably.

---

### Option 3: Credit card (pay off next month)

**How it works:**
Buy on credit card. Pay full amount next month. Zero interest if paid in full.

**Pros:**
✅ Get it now
✅ Zero interest if paid in 30 days
✅ May get cashback

**Cons:**
❌ Need full amount in 30 days
❌ If you can't pay full, interest is brutal (36-42%)
❌ Risk of debt spiral

**Best for:** When you have money but it's locked (FD, waiting for salary, etc.)

---

### Option 4: 0% EMI offers

**How it works:**
"Zero interest EMI" — split cost over months, no interest charged.

**Reality check:**
Often the "no interest" is built into higher price. Compare:
- Cash price: ₹50,000
- 0% EMI price: ₹52,000 (₹2,000 hidden as "processing fee")

**Sometimes it's genuine. Sometimes it's not.**

## Decision framework: Save vs EMI

### When to save and buy:

✅ You can wait 2-3 months
✅ Saving won't hurt your emergency fund
✅ Interest rate on EMI is high (15%+)
✅ You have the discipline to save consistently

**Example:**
Need ₹40,000 phone. Earn ₹45,000. Can save ₹8,000/month for 5 months. Just save and buy.

---

### When EMI might make sense:

✅ Urgent need (laptop for work)
✅ Low interest rate (8-10%)
✅ EMI fits comfortably in budget
✅ You'd save the money anyway (might as well use it monthly)

**Example:**
Laptop died. Need it for work. ₹60,000. Earn ₹50,000. Can afford ₹5,000 EMI easily. Take 12-month plan.

## Real math examples

### Example 1: ₹60,000 laptop

**Scenario:** Earning ₹40,000/month, rent ₹18,000

**Option A: Save for 3 months**
- Save ₹8,000/month × 3 = ₹24,000
- Use from savings: ₹36,000
- Buy ₹60,000 laptop
- **Cost:** ₹60,000
- **Time:** 3 months

**Option B: EMI**
- ₹60,000 at 14% interest for 12 months
- EMI: ₹5,360/month
- **Total paid:** ₹64,320
- **Extra cost:** ₹4,320 (interest)
- Get laptop now, but pay ₹4,320 more

**OneStep's take:** With ₹18k rent on ₹40k salary, ₹5,360 EMI is tight. Save for 3 months instead. The ₹4,320 interest isn't worth it.

---

### Example 2: ₹40,000 phone

**Scenario:** Earning ₹50,000/month, rent ₹12,000, have ₹30,000 saved

**Option A: Pay from savings**
- Use ₹30,000 saved + ₹10,000 from salary
- Buy phone
- **Cost:** ₹40,000
- Emergency fund reduced temporarily

**Option B: Credit card (pay next month)**
- Buy on card
- Pay ₹40,000 next month from salary + savings
- **Cost:** ₹40,000 (zero interest if paid in full)
- Get cashback (₹400-800)

**OneStep's take:** Option B (credit card). You have the money. Use card for cashback. Pay full amount next month. Zero interest, small benefit.

## Common big purchase mistakes

### ❌ Mistake 1: Buying on impulse

**What happens:**
See sale. "50% off!" Buy ₹50,000 thing you don't need.

Regret later. Either can't afford EMI or savings wiped out.

**Better approach:**
Sleep on it for 2-3 days. Still need it? Then plan to buy.

---

### ❌ Mistake 2: Only looking at EMI amount

**What happens:**
"₹3,000 EMI? I can afford that!"

Ignore total cost. ₹35,000 item becomes ₹42,000 with interest.

**Better approach:**
Calculate total cost. Is the interest worth getting it now?

---

### ❌ Mistake 3: Maxing out credit card

**What happens:**
₹50,000 limit. Spend ₹48,000. Can't pay full amount. Interest piles up.

**Better approach:**
Only use credit card if you can pay FULL amount next month. Otherwise, don't.

---

### ❌ Mistake 4: Not having emergency buffer

**What happens:**
Use all savings for purchase. Emergency happens. No money. Take high-interest loan.

**Better approach:**
Keep ₹15,000-20,000 as emergency buffer. Don't touch it for purchases.

## The questions you're too embarrassed to ask

### But shouldn't be embarrassed about

**"What even is HRA?"**
House Rent Allowance. Part of your salary meant to help cover rent. You can claim tax deductions on it if you're paying rent.

**"Why is my salary less than what they offered?"**
They told you CTC (Cost to Company). You get in-hand salary (after deductions like tax, PF).

**"What's the difference between saving and investing?"**
Saving = money sits safely in bank. Grows slowly.
Investing = money goes into stocks/mutual funds. Can grow faster but has risk.

**"How does EMI work?"**
You're splitting a big payment into smaller monthly chunks. But you pay interest, so total cost is higher.

**"What's a credit score?"**
A number (300-900) that shows if you're good at paying bills on time. Banks check this before giving loans.

**See? Not complicated. Just not explained clearly before.**

## Start here: The absolute basics

### 💰 Understanding your money

**Your salary has two numbers:**

**CTC (Cost to Company):**
What the company spends on you. Includes your salary + benefits + their contributions.

**In-hand salary:**
What actually hits your bank account after all deductions.

**Example:**
CTC: ₹6 lakhs per year = ₹50,000/month
In-hand: Maybe ₹42,000/month (after PF, tax, etc.)

**The in-hand number is what you actually budget with.**

---

### 🏦 Where your money goes

**Deductions you'll see on salary slip:**

**PF (Provident Fund):** ₹1,800-2,500/month
- Retirement savings. Locked until you're 58 or quit job.
- Your company matches this amount.

**Professional Tax:** ₹200/month
- State tax. You can't avoid it.

**TDS (Tax Deducted at Source):** Varies
- Income tax deducted before salary hits you.
- Depends on your total salary.

**After all this → In-hand salary (what you actually get).**

---

### 💳 Basic financial products explained

**Savings account:**
Where your salary comes. Earns tiny interest (3-4%). Money available anytime.

**Credit card:**
Borrow money from bank for 30 days. Pay back in full = zero interest. Pay partially = very high interest (36-40%).

**Personal loan:**
Bank gives you lump sum. You pay back in monthly EMIs with interest.

**Fixed Deposit (FD):**
Lock money for 6 months-5 years. Get fixed interest (6-7%). Can't access easily.

**Mutual fund:**
Pool money with others. Professional invests in stocks. Can gain or lose value.

## Financial terms translated to plain language

| They say | Actually means |
|----------|----------------|
| APR | Interest rate per year |
| EMI | Monthly installment payment |
| Credit utilization | How much of credit card limit you're using |
| Liquid funds | Savings you can access quickly |
| Debt-to-income ratio | How much you owe vs how much you earn |
| Amortization | How loan gets paid off over time |
| Compounding | Interest earning interest (grows faster over time) |
| Net worth | What you own minus what you owe |

## Common beginner questions answered

### "How much should I save?"

**Generic advice:** "50% of salary"
**Reality:** Save what you can after covering basics.

- Earning ₹30k, rent ₹15k → Save ₹3,000-4,000 (10-13%)
- Earning ₹50k, rent ₹12k → Save ₹8,000-12,000 (16-24%)

**Whatever you can save consistently is the right amount.**

---

### "Should I invest or just save?"

**First year:** Focus on saving. Build emergency fund (₹15,000-30,000).

**After emergency fund exists:** Then explore basic investing.

**Don't invest before emergency fund is built.** You need accessible cash first.

---

### "Do I need a credit card?"

**You need it if:** You can commit to paying full bill every month.

**You don't need it if:** You're not sure you can pay it off monthly.

Credit cards are useful for building credit history. But only if used responsibly.

---

### "What's a good salary?"

**There's no universal answer.**

₹30,000 in Tier-2 city = comfortable
₹30,000 in Mumbai = very tight

₹50,000 in Bangalore with no rent = great
₹50,000 in Bangalore with ₹20k rent = okay

**"Good" depends on your expenses and location.**

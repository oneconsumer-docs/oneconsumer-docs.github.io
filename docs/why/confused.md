## The most confusing financial terms (explained simply)

### APR (Annual Percentage Rate)

**What they say:** "Our APR is 12%."

**What it means:** If you borrow money, you'll pay 12% extra per year. ₹10,000 borrowed = ₹1,200 interest in a year.

### EMI (Equated Monthly Installment)

**What they say:** "Just pay ₹5,000 EMI for 12 months."

**What it means:** You're breaking a big purchase (₹60,000) into 12 smaller monthly payments (₹5,000 each). But you're also paying interest, so the total might be ₹65,000.

### Credit Score

**What they say:** "Maintain a good credit score."

**What it means:** A number (300-900) that shows whether you pay bills on time. Higher is better. Lenders check this before giving you loans.

### Credit Utilization

**What they say:** "Keep your credit utilization under 30%."

**What it means:** If your credit card limit is ₹50,000, don't use more than ₹15,000 at a time. Using too much makes lenders nervous.

### Processing Fee

**What they say:** "Just a small processing fee."

**What it means:** An extra charge (usually 1-3% of the loan amount) for "processing" your application. On a ₹1 lakh loan, that's ₹1,000-3,000 extra.

### Compound Interest

**What they say:** "Interest is compounded monthly."

**What it means:** Interest on top of interest. The longer you wait to pay, the more you owe. This is why credit card debt grows so fast.

## The questions you're too embarrassed to ask

**"What even is HRA?"**
House Rent Allowance. Part of your salary meant to help cover rent. It's tax-deductible if you're paying rent.

**"Why is my salary slip number higher than what I actually got?"**
That's gross salary (before deductions). You get net salary (after taxes, PF, etc).

**"Is ₹5,000 savings enough?"**
If that's what you can save after covering basics, then yes. Saving something is better than saving nothing.

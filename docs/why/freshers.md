## What to do first (the actual first steps)

### Step 1: Don't panic spend

That first salary feels like freedom. You want to buy everything you've been holding back on.

**Hold on.** Take a breath. You have time.

### Step 2: Understand what you actually got

Look at your salary slip. See that number that says "gross salary"? That's not what you got. Look for "net salary" or "in-hand salary." That's your actual money.

**Common deductions you'll see:**
- **PF (Provident Fund)**: Goes to your retirement savings
- **Professional Tax**: State tax
- **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)**: Income tax
- **HRA, DA, other allowances**: Part of your salary structure

[Chat with OneStep to understand your salary slip →](https://oneconsumer.money)

### Step 3: Set aside money for basics first

Before anything else, cover these:

1. **Rent** (if applicable)
2. **Food** (groceries, meals)
3. **Transport** (metro, cab, fuel)
4. **Bills** (phone, internet)

Whatever's left is what you actually have to work with.

### Step 4: Save _something_ (even if it's small)

You don't need to save 50%. Even ₹2,000 is a start.

**Why?** Because you're building a habit. And habits matter more than amounts when you're starting out.

---

## The 4 buckets for your first salary

Once you've covered basics, think about these buckets:

### 1. **Emergency fund** (Start here)
Life happens. Phone breaks. Medical emergency. Unexpected expense.

**Goal:** Build up 2-3 months of basic expenses over time.
**Start with:** Even ₹1,000-2,000 per month.

### 2. **Savings for big purchases**
That laptop you need. New phone. Trip home.

**Put aside money each month** so you're not scrambling when you need it.

### 3. **Investments** (Optional for now)
This can wait if money's tight. Focus on emergency fund first.

When you're ready, **ask OneStep** about where to start.

### 4. **Enjoy money**
Yes, really. You earned it. You can enjoy some of it.

**Budget for fun.** Movies, meals out, hobbies. Life's not just about saving.

## Common first-salary questions

**"Should I get a credit card?"**

Maybe! It can help build credit history. But only if you can pay it off every month. If you're not sure, ask OneStep.

[Should I get a credit card? →]({{ site.baseurl }}/docs/why/credit-cards/)

**"Should I invest my first salary?"**

Not all of it. Get your emergency fund started first. Then think about investing.

**"How much should I save?"**

Whatever you can. There's no magic number. Even 10-15% is great if that's what fits your budget.

**"Can I afford to eat out / buy that thing / take that trip?"**

Ask yourself: Did I cover basics? Did I save something? Do I have money left over?

If yes to all three, then yes, you can afford it.

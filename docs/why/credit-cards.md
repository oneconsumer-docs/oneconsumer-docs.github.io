**The honest answer: it depends.**

Credit cards aren't good or bad. They're a tool. Used right, they help you build credit history and manage cash flow. Used wrong, they trap you in debt.

**So should YOU get one?** Let's figure it out.

---

## When a credit card makes sense

### ✅ You can pay it off every month

This is the golden rule. If you can't pay the full amount every month, **don't get a credit card.**

Credit card interest rates in India are brutal — usually 36-42% per year. That ₹5,000 purchase becomes ₹7,000 real fast if you're only paying the minimum.

### ✅ You want to build credit history

In India, having a credit history matters. When you want a car loan, home loan, or even rent an apartment, lenders check your credit score.

A credit card used responsibly (pay on time, don't max it out) builds that history.

### ✅ You're okay with the responsibility

Credit cards require discipline. Swiping feels easy. Paying the bill feels hard.

If you trust yourself to track spending and pay bills on time, you're ready.

## When you should wait

### ❌ You're already struggling with expenses

If your salary barely covers rent, food, and basics, a credit card won't help. It'll just add stress.

**Get your budget stable first.** Then think about a credit card.

### ❌ You're not sure you'll pay it off

Be honest with yourself. If there's any chance you'll carry a balance month-to-month, **wait.**

Debt compounds fast. Don't learn that the hard way.

## What to know before getting your first card

### How credit cards actually work

1. You use the card to make purchases
2. The bank pays the merchant
3. You get a bill at the end of the month
4. If you pay the **full amount** by the due date, you pay **zero interest**
5. If you pay only part of it, you get charged **very high interest** on the remaining balance

**Key point:** Pay the full bill. Every time. No exceptions.

### What to look for in your first card

**Zero or low annual fee**
Many starter cards have no annual fee. Don't pay ₹500-1,000 yearly for a card you're just learning to use.

**Simple rewards**
Cashback is easier to understand than reward points. Look for cards that give 1-2% cashback.

**Low credit limit**
Actually a good thing when you're starting. A ₹20,000-50,000 limit keeps you from overspending.

**Good customer support**
You'll have questions. Pick a bank with responsive support.

## Common credit card myths (busted)

**Myth: "I should only pay the minimum due"**
**Truth:** Minimum due is a trap. Pay the full amount. Always.

**Myth: "More cards = better credit score"**
**Truth:** Quality over quantity. One card used responsibly is better than three you can't manage.

**Myth: "I should use my full credit limit to build credit"**
**Truth:** Keep usage under 30% of your limit. Using ₹50,000 of a ₹50,000 limit hurts your score.

**Myth: "Closing old cards helps my credit"**
**Truth:** Older cards show longer credit history. Keep them open (but don't use if they have fees).

## How to use a credit card responsibly

### Rule 1: Treat it like a debit card

Only buy what you already have money for. Credit card is **not extra money**. It's borrowed money that you pay back in 30 days.

### Rule 2: Pay the full bill. Every single month.

Set a calendar reminder for 3 days before the due date. Automate payment if you can.

**Never, ever just pay the minimum.**

### Rule 3: Track your spending

Check your transactions weekly. Make sure everything is yours. Catch fraudulent charges early.

### Rule 4: Don't give out your CVV or OTP

Legitimate companies never ask for this. If someone does, **it's a scam.**

## Choosing your first card

Once you've decided you're ready, the next question is: which card?

There are hundreds of options. Cashback cards. Travel cards. Fuel cards. Rewards cards. Shopping cards.

**For your first card, keep it simple:**

- Zero or low annual fee
- Basic cashback (1-2%)
- From a bank you trust
- Low starting limit

**Should you get a credit card?**
